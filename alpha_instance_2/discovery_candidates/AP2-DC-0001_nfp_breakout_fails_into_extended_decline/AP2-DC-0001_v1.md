## 1. Observation

On 2024-08-02 (a Friday, the first Friday of the month), immediately following an ordinary quiet
grind (baseline volume ~3000-5200, ranges ~1-4pt), the M15 chart showed a four-phase sequence:

- **12:30 UTC** -- sharp breakout: O2455.6 H2477.74 L2455.58 C2471 (22.14pt range), volume 12627
  (~2.5-4x the immediate baseline). Followed by 12:45 UTC continuing higher (H2477.46, C2472.91,
  vol 11308), holding near the highs.
- **13:00-13:45 UTC** -- reversal begins: 13:00 UTC gives back most of the gain (O2472.9 L2460.87
  C2461.57, vol 11829), then oscillates with continued high volume (10219-11308) between roughly
  2461 and 2472 for several candles, including one partial recovery back to 2471.96.
- **14:30-15:00 UTC** -- a second, much larger leg down: 14:30 UTC O2464.58 H2465.66 L2434.15
  C2436.73 (31.5pt range, the largest single candle in the whole sequence), vol 12423. Continues
  15:00 UTC to L2411.59 C2413.59, vol 11359 -- undershooting well below the pre-breakout origin
  (~2455-2465) and well below the intraday low that preceded the original breakout.
- **15:15-17:15 UTC** -- partial recovery and stabilization: bounces to C2426.1 (vol 10335), then
  volume steadily decays back toward baseline (10335 -> 8981 -> ... -> 2759-6351) as price
  consolidates in a 2427-2433 range -- well below both the breakout peak (2477.74) and the
  pre-event baseline (2455-2465), but above the extreme low (2411.59).

Net effect: an upside breakout that fully fails, followed by a decline roughly 40% larger in
magnitude than the original breakout, with the instrument settling materially lower than where it
traded before the entire sequence began.

## 2. Why It Attracted Attention

This is, by a wide margin, the largest and most sustained volatility event observed by this
instance so far (compare: the two prior Observation Registry entries topped out at ~15pt ranges
and ~11700 peak volume over at most ~3.5 hours; this event produced a 31.5pt single-candle range,
a peak volume of 12627, and roughly 5 hours of continuously-elevated volume (2-4x baseline
throughout, not just at the peak). The shape is also structurally distinct from anything else this
instance has logged: not a simple spike-and-fade back to the origin, and not two-way chop that
nets out flat -- this is a directional failure (breakout up) immediately followed by a much larger
move in the opposite direction that overshoots the pre-event range entirely.

The timing (12:30 UTC on the first Friday of the month) is a mechanically verifiable calendar
fact -- the U.S. non-farm payrolls report is scheduled at 12:30 UTC (8:30 AM ET) on the first
Friday of most months, and 2024-08-02 fits that pattern. This is noted as a candidate timing
regularity worth tracking, not as a confirmed causal claim -- this instance has not examined
whether the mechanism generalizes to other first-Friday sessions, and does not know how many
similar sessions exist elsewhere in the authorized period.

## 3. Why It May Repeat

If the initial breakout is genuinely tied to a recurring scheduled calendar event (a monthly
data release), the *opportunity* for a similarly-timed volatility burst recurs monthly by
construction. Independent of any specific calendar link, "sharp breakout immediately fails and
the reversal extends beyond the pre-breakout level" is a directional-failure pattern that is not
inherently tied to any one instrument or date -- it is a plausible, generic exhaustion/failed-
breakout mechanism that should in principle be checkable against other large-volume expansion
events in this same replay, calendar-linked or not.

## 4. Why It Deserves Further Investigation

The magnitude and duration of sustained elevated volume (roughly 5 hours, 2-4x baseline
throughout, not a single-candle artifact) argue against this being noise or a data anomaly. The
clean four-phase structure (breakout -> partial hold -> larger reversal -> stabilization at a net-
lower level) is distinctive enough that future instances of large expansion candles in this
replay should be checked against it: does a breakout of this scale reliably fail, or was this
particular instance's outcome a coincidence? Does the "second leg exceeds the first" property
(decline ~40% larger than the initial breakout) recur, or was this specific ratio incidental?
Whether this is calendar-linked (first-Friday/NFP-adjacent) or a general large-breakout-failure
property is itself an open question worth resolving with more instances.

## 5. Confidence

Medium. The event itself is unambiguous and large (not a borderline judgment call), but this is a
single instance with no repetition yet observed within this instance's own history, and the
calendar-timing observation is noted as a candidate, unconfirmed factor rather than an established
cause.
