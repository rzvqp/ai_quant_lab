# RED TEAM — CRITIQUE BATTERY v1.0
### The standing quality-control checklist every Discovery Candidate receives
**Status:** ✅ v1.0 — **RATIFIED** by CEO decision, 2026-07-21. Operational.
**Parent:** [CHARTER.md](../CHARTER.md) §8, §9. **Version binding:** every review records that it ran CRITIQUE_BATTERY v1.0.

> **What this battery IS:** a simple, consistent checklist that every Discovery Candidate receives before moving deeper into the laboratory.
> **What it is NOT:** a validation methodology, a statistical framework, a research program, or a replacement for Flow C.
> **Objective:** *quality control, not candidate destruction.*

---

## Operating stance

- **Simple, consistent, objective, repeatable, lightweight.** Five questions, three verdicts. Nothing heavier.
- **Assume Alpha acts in good faith.** The battery evaluates the *submitted Discovery Candidate*, never Alpha.
- **Evaluate fairly, not aggressively.** The battery must not become an obstacle that rejects nearly every candidate. A reasonable, clearly-described observation with honest evidence should pass.
- **Submitted evidence only.** No experiments, no statistics, no searching for additional data, no reproduction, no improving the candidate.
- The five critiques are **read** against the submitted package and answered plainly. They feed one decision: *is this worth further laboratory resources?*

---

## The five critiques

### C1 — Observation Quality
**Is the observation clearly described and objectively understandable?**
The goal is **clarity, not correctness.** Can a neutral reader understand what was observed, on what instrument/timeframe/window, without guessing? A vague or ambiguous observation fails C1 even if it might be true.

### C2 — Evidence Quality
**Do the submitted observations support the stated Discovery Candidate?**
Evaluate **only the submitted evidence.** Do **not** search for additional evidence. Ask whether what is in the package actually backs the candidate as written — not whether more evidence exists somewhere.

### C3 — Alternative Explanation
**Is there at least one plausible alternative explanation that should remain open?**
The alternative **does not need to be proven — only identified.** Naming an open alternative is normal and healthy; it does not by itself sink a candidate. It is recorded so the candidate is not treated as the only possible reading.

### C4 — Claim Discipline
**Does the Discovery Candidate remain descriptive, or does it claim more than the evidence supports?**
Discovery Candidates should **avoid causal conclusions.** A description of *what was observed* is disciplined; a claim about *why it happens* or *that it will persist* usually exceeds a Discovery Candidate's remit.

### C5 — Worth Investigating
**Based on the submission alone, does this Discovery Candidate deserve additional laboratory resources?**
This is **not** "Is it true?" — it **is** "Is it worth investigating?" This is the pivotal question the verdict turns on.

---

## Verdicts (exactly three)

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **CONTINUE INVESTIGATION** | The Discovery Candidate deserves further investigation. |
| 🟡 **NEEDS BETTER EVIDENCE** | Interesting observation; current evidence is insufficient. May be resubmitted later. |
| 🔴 **NOT RECOMMENDED** | The current submission does not justify further laboratory resources. |

### How the five critiques map to a verdict (guidance, not a rigid formula)
- 🟢 **CONTINUE** — C1 clear, C2 the submitted evidence supports the candidate, C4 stays descriptive, and C5 = yes. An open C3 alternative is fine; it is simply noted.
- 🟡 **NEEDS BETTER EVIDENCE** — the observation is clear and genuinely interesting (C1 ok, C5 leans yes) but C2 is thin: the submitted evidence does not yet support the candidate. Not wrong — just not shown yet. Invite resubmission with stronger evidence.
- 🔴 **NOT RECOMMENDED** — C5 = no: the submission, on its own terms, does not justify further resources (e.g. the observation is unclear beyond rescue at C1, the claim badly overreaches the evidence at C4 with no descriptive core to keep, or it is simply not worth the laboratory's resources).

The reviewer records a one-line answer to each of C1–C5, then the single verdict with a one-line reason. That is the whole battery.

---

## Important rules (bounds of the battery)

The Critique Battery:
- evaluates the **Discovery Candidate** — it does **NOT** evaluate Alpha;
- does **NOT** validate the market;
- does **NOT** run experiments;
- does **NOT** produce statistics;
- does **NOT** search for additional data;
- does **NOT** improve the candidate.

It only evaluates the **quality of the submitted Discovery Candidate.**

## Note on future revision
Per CEO direction, v1.0 is intentionally minimal — built for practical review experience, not theoretical completeness. After several real reviews, the battery will be revisited and formalized based on **observed review patterns**. Any change is a version bump and requires explicit CEO ratification.
