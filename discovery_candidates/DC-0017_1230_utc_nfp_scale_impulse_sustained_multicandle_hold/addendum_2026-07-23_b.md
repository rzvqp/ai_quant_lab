## New Observation

On 2025-09-11 (a Thursday, not a first-Friday NFP date), the 12:30-12:45 UTC M15 candle ran
O3624.27 H3644.085 L3612.835 C3637.04, a ~31.25-point range, volume 25,399 -- the second-highest
single-candle volume observed at the 12:30 UTC window in this replay, after DC-0017's original
NFP-scale instance (30,975).

Unlike DC-0017's original instance, which held its gains across four subsequent candles without
reversing, this instance resolved as extended two-sided chop: over the following nine M15 candles
(12:45-15:00 UTC), price oscillated repeatedly between roughly 3612.8 and 3644 with no sustained
directional resolution, closing near 3629.5 by 15:00 UTC -- close to where the impulse candle had
opened (3624.27), effectively giving back nearly all of the initial move over the following two
hours. Volume declined gradually and somewhat unevenly over this span (20001, 17356, 15244, 19256,
14685, 14069, 11274, 9809, 9659) rather than in a clean monotonic decay.

## Why This Matters To DC-0017

DC-0017 raised the question of whether "impulse then hold" is characteristic of 12:30 UTC releases
specifically, or specific to NFP-scale events. This instance -- the second-largest 12:30 UTC volume
observed, but on a non-NFP date -- resolved in a third distinct way: neither a clean hold (DC-0017)
nor a directional failure-and-decline (DC-0018's unrelated 14:00 UTC instance), but extended
two-sided chop that ultimately gave back the entire initial move. This is a data point against
"large 12:30 UTC volume predicts a hold/continuation" -- magnitude alone did not determine the
resolution shape here. Whether this instance corresponded to a different scheduled release (e.g.
CPI or another macro print, as opposed to NFP) is not established from price/volume data alone.
