## New Observation

Continuing to observe past the four-candle window documented in `candidate_v1.md` (which covered
12:30-13:45 UTC), the elevated volume actually persisted far longer than originally captured.
Per-M15-candle volume from 13:45 through 16:45 UTC (13 further candles): 21722, 19619, 20556,
22033, 18928, 15065, 11238, 11994, 11259, 9209, 10665, 8932 -- a gradual decay from the original
30,975 spike down toward (but not fully back to) the pre-impulse baseline of roughly 4,000-8,000
seen earlier that morning, taking approximately 4 hours 15 minutes in total (17 M15 candles from
12:30 to 16:45 UTC) to substantially normalize.

Price behavior over this extended tail was not flat consolidation: rather than merely holding the
initial impulse's 3572-3587 range as described in the original candidate, price continued to
grind higher through the tail, reaching a marginal new high of 3600.205 around 16:30 UTC before
giving back a small amount into the 3592-3595 area by 16:45 UTC. The net move from the pre-impulse
open (3555.235) to the point of substantial volume normalization (~3595) is roughly 40 points,
achieved gradually over the full ~4h15m window rather than concentrated in the initial spike alone.

## Why This Matters To DC-0017

DC-0017's original observation window (12:30-13:45 UTC, four candles) undersold the actual duration
of the elevated-volume regime and characterized the post-impulse behavior as "holding" a range. The
fuller picture is more nuanced: volume decayed gradually over roughly 4h15m rather than settling
quickly, and price continued to drift higher (not sideways) during that decay, adding a further
~7-9 points beyond the initial impulse's close before volume substantially normalized. This
resembles, in a stretched-out and lower-intensity form, some of the gradual-build character of the
DC-0013/DC-0015/DC-0016 "large sustained expansion" family, suggesting that NFP-scale impulses may
transition into an extended lower-grade continuation phase rather than a clean binary of
"hold" vs. "reverse" -- though this rests on a single instance and should not be generalized.
