## New Observation: A Second, Later Retest Fails Where The First Held

Roughly 22 hours after the break documented in Addendum B, and about 21 hours after the first
post-break retest documented in Addendum C (which reclaimed above the old band), price returned to
the same band a second time and this time fell through it:

- **2025-08-05 09:30 UTC** — low 3362.15, dipping into the old band (3358-3363.6), close 3363.67.
- **2025-08-05 09:45 UTC** — low 3358.955, essentially at the old band's lower edge, close 3359.66,
  volume 4,039.
- **2025-08-05 10:00 UTC** — decisive break below the band: high 3361.79, low 3354.87, close
  3356.735, volume 3,700.
- **2025-08-05 10:15 UTC** — held below, no reclaim attempt: high 3358.215 (still under the old
  band ceiling), close 3356.355, volume 3,188.

Unlike the first retest (Addendum C, 2025-08-04 12:30 UTC), which touched the top of the old band
and reclaimed within roughly 15 minutes, this second retest pushed all the way through the band and
stayed below it for at least two consecutive M15 candles.

## Why This Matters To DC-0009

This directly contradicts a naive reading of Addendum C as "the old resistance now reliably acts as
support." The same band held as support on its first retest and failed on its second, about a day
later. Descriptively, this suggests that "broken resistance becomes support" is not a durable,
one-time-established property of a level — subsequent retests of the same broken level are not
guaranteed to behave the same way as the first. No explanation for the difference (time elapsed,
approach speed, broader trend context) is offered here; this addendum only files the contradiction.
