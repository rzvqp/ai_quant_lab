## New Observation

One calendar day later, 2025-09-02, at almost the identical clock-time window: starting at the
01:00-01:15 UTC M15 candle (O3479.21), price advanced across three consecutive M15 candles to a
peak high of 3508.79 within the 01:30-01:45 candle (O3502.76, H3508.79, L3492.505, C3493.94 —
a large intra-candle wick and reversal, volume 20353), before pulling back and consolidating over
the next two candles (volume declining to 14470 then 5814, range narrowing to roughly 3491-3495).

The point move for this instance was approximately 29.6 points (3479.21 -> 3508.79) over roughly
45 minutes (3 candles) -- smaller in magnitude and shorter in duration than DC-0016's original
instance (~47.2pt over ~6-7 candles / ~1h45m), but sharing the same construction (volume climbing
across consecutive candles: 10371, 12031, 9193, 20353) and the same ending shape (a marginal/local
high reached on the highest-volume candle, immediately followed by a sharp intra-candle or
next-candle reversal). Dropping to M5 on the 20353-volume candle showed distribution across the
three M5 sub-candles (7278/6713/6362), none exceeding roughly 36% of the total -- the same
"sustained, distributed" construction as DC-0008/DC-0013/DC-0014/DC-0015/DC-0016.

## Why This Matters To DC-0016

DC-0016's Additional Notes explicitly flagged that its ~47.2pt magnitude and ~1h45m duration rested
on a single instance at this clock-time window, and that comparing future instances at the same
hour would be a natural next step. This is that next instance: same hour (01:00-02:15 UTC region),
same session context (Monday->Tuesday, immediately following an ordinary 00:00 UTC volume uptick,
itself already within the documented range for that window), same underlying construction, and the
same "marginal-high-then-reversal" ending shape -- but at roughly 60% of the point magnitude and
under half the duration. This is a data point suggesting the ending shape (reversal at a marginal
new high, rather than consolidation or modest pullback) may recur more reliably at this specific
hour than the magnitude or duration do, though n=2 for this hour is far too small to treat that as
established. It is also a data point against the ~47.2pt magnitude being a fixed or typical size
for this hour's expansions -- a second instance at roughly 63% of that size suggests real
variability rather than a fixed magnitude.
